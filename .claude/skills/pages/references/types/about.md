<page_type name="about" mode="persuasion" goals="credibility transfer" nav="full">

<job>
Who these people are and why to trust them. Readers arrive sceptical, close to choosing, and see
through corporate language and stock photographs; a real place and visible people are what make a
business credible. It is on the path to buying, so it ends in the action. Many readers skip it, so
its trust facts also belong on the homepage and service pages.

Contact and locations pages have their own types now (`contact.md`, `location.md`); the team page
is `team.md`.
</job>

<angle>
Decide per page and write in the brief's frontmatter, from this brand's own material.

  reader  someone close to choosing, checking the company out.
  pain    the doubt they hold: are these real people, are they local, will they answer.
  offer   what the people bring: experience, qualifications, being the ones who show up.
  lead    usually what the company does, then the people who do it.
</angle>

<inventory>
Sort every usable item into bins, with its source.

  people      names and roles as the brand states them; staff named in reviews, with the role
              the reviewer gives
  history     founding, years, why the company exists, in their own words
  beliefs     what they value, only where an action or a credential proves it
  place       address, premises photograph, the area they are from and work in
  credentials licences, certifications, memberships, as marks and numbers
  proof       third-party reviews, especially those that name the people
</inventory>

<select>
  - What the company does comes first, in a line or two.
  - Named people with real photographs beat a values statement; a belief proven by an action or
    credential beats a belief stated.
  - Facts a reader could check beat adjectives. Cut mission-speak any company could say.
  - Reviews that name staff are the strongest proof this page can carry.
  - At most one lead figure: the number that best answers the reader's pain, with its source,
    named in the brief. The design system decides how it is emphasised; with no such number,
    nothing is emphasised.
  - Reviews: several sit together in their own band; one may also sit beside the claim it
    proves. The design system decides how either looks. Each carries its platform's mark and
    its stars in the brief (SKILL.md, "Proof, on every page").
</select>

<sections>
In page order. `core` is on nearly every page of this type; everything else is a choice made
against the material. Each line says what the band holds, then when it earns its place.

  what        core      What the company does and for whom, in a line or two. The reader should not
                        have to read further to know.
  people      core      Who they are: named, with a photograph if the brand has one. With many
                        staff, a preview linking to the team page.
  story       optional  Why the company exists and when it started, in their words, short.
                        When: it is true, specific, and says something only they could.
  facts       optional  The checkable numbers: years trading, jobs done, clients, locations, with sources.
                        When: the brand states them.
  approach    optional  How they work, each point backed by a practice or credential.
                        When: every point has something behind it. Values with nothing behind them are cut.
  credentials  optional  Licences, certifications, memberships, awards, as marks and numbers.
                        When: the brand holds them. Core for licensed or regulated work.
  place       optional  Where they are: address, premises photograph, the area they are from.
                        When: being local or having premises is part of the reason to trust them.
  community   optional  What they do locally, with specifics. When: the brand records it.
  proof       optional  Third-party reviews, especially ones naming the people, with platform.
                        When: they exist.
  action      core      The action, and what happens after it.
</sections>

<phrase>
  - Headings answer "who are these people and why trust them", not "About us".
  - People are named as the brand names them; a role comes from the brand or from a review that
    states it, never guessed. A bio may be personal where the brand's own material is.
  - Plain language; no jargon.
  - Everything in this file describes a shape. Never reuse its wording, or another page's.
</phrase>

<show>
  - Portraits of the actual people: readers look at them longer than at the bios.
  - The premises and vehicles as evidence the company is real and local.
  - No stock photographs of people, ever; with no team photograph, set the names in type.
</show>

<links>
To: contact, the services, and one page per person where there are many staff. From: the homepage
and the footer.
</links>

<industry>
Clinics and professional services: the practitioner is what is bought, so bios with qualifications
and approach carry the page. Trades: the people who turn up, and the licence. Software: how the
company operates and keeps data safe matters more than leadership. Regulated work carries what the
law requires (industries.md).
</industry>

</page_type>
