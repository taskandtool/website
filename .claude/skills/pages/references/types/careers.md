<page_type name="careers" mode="persuasion" goals="one" nav="full">

<job>
A candidate is deciding whether to apply. They want to know what the open roles are, what the work
and the pay are like, and how to apply. An honest "no openings right now" with a way to get in touch
beats a stale posting. A careers page that is only a link to an applicant system gives no reason to
apply.
</job>

<angle>
Decide per page and write in the brief's frontmatter, from this brand's own material.

  reader  the person the company most needs to hire, and what they are leaving.
  pain    is this a good place to work, and will it pay.
  offer   what the company actually offers: pay, hours, training, the people, the work itself.
  lead    usually the open roles; with none, what working here is like, then how to apply anyway.
</angle>

<inventory>
  roles       each open role: title, place or remote, type, pay range, what it involves, how to apply
  offer       pay, benefits, hours, training, equipment, as the brand states them
  people      staff who can say what it is like, by name, with their consent
  process     what happens after someone applies
</inventory>

<select>
  - Real pay ranges where the brand states them, or where the law requires them.
  - Benefits the brand actually offers, named; no culture adjectives.
  - Only current roles, dated.
</select>

<sections>
In page order. `core` is on nearly every careers page; everything else is a choice made against
the material. Each line says what the band holds, then when it earns its place.

  roles       core      Each open role with title, place, type, pay and a link to apply; or an honest
                        line that none are open, and how to get in touch anyway.
  offer       optional  Pay, benefits, hours, training. When: the brand states them.
  people      optional  Staff in their own words, named. When: the brand has them.
  process     optional  What happens after applying, and how long it takes. When: the brand says.
  apply       core      How to apply, with what to send.
</sections>

<phrase>
  - Role titles as the trade or profession names them, not invented ones.
  - Everything in this file describes a shape. Never reuse its wording, or another page's.
</phrase>

<show>
  - The actual team at work, where the business's photographs have it. Never stock.
</show>

<links>
From: the footer, and the about page. To: each role's page or the application.
</links>

<variant name="role" label="one job posting" mode="retrieval">
The title, place or remote, type, pay, the date posted and a closing date, what the job involves,
what is needed, and how to apply. The page is removed or marked closed when the role is filled.
</variant>

</page_type>
