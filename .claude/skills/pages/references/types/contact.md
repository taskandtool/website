<page_type name="contact" mode="retrieval" goals="reach them" nav="full">

<job>
The reader has chosen, or is stuck, and needs the right way to reach the company, fast. They expect
a dedicated contact page as well as the phone number in the header or footer. A page that offers
only a form, hides the phone number, or gives no hours or reply time fails them.
</job>

<reader>
Who is getting in touch and why: a new customer ready to book, an existing one with a problem,
someone who needs directions. Which channel each one wants, and whether any of it is urgent.
</reader>

<inventory>
  channels    phone numbers (which one is main), email, text, booking link, chat if staffed
  when        hours, holiday hours, emergency or after-hours cover, how fast they reply
  where       full address with postcode, parking, transit, entrance; or the areas served
  who         who answers, by purpose (sales, service, billing), where the business has them
  next        what happens after they call or send the form
</inventory>

<select>
  - Phone and email in plain text, as links, never behind a form.
  - One main number first. Other numbers are grouped by what the reader needs, not by department.
  - Hours and the reply time are stated when the brand states them.
  - The form is an addition, three to five fields, each describing the reader's need. No account.
  - A service-area business names the places it serves and does not publish a home address.
  - Only channels someone answers. Unmonitored social links and chat are cut.
</select>

<sections>
In page order. `core` is on nearly every contact page; everything else is a choice made against
the material. Each line says what the band holds, then when it earns its place.

  reach       core      The main phone number and email as links, with hours and how fast they reply.
  urgent      optional  The emergency or after-hours line and what it covers.
                        When: the brand offers one. Then it sits beside the main number.
  by purpose  optional  Other numbers or addresses, each labelled by what the reader needs.
                        When: the business has more than one real channel.
  form        optional  A short enquiry or booking form, with what happens after it is sent.
                        When: the brand takes enquiries this way.
  visit       optional  Full address, map link, parking, transit, entrance.
                        When: customers come to them. A list of places instead for a service area.
  locations   optional  Each location's address, phone and hours, linking to its page.
                        When: there is more than one location.
  next        core      One line on what happens after they get in touch, when the brand says.
</sections>

<phrase>
  - Labels and values, not prose. A number is a number, not a sentence about calling.
  - Invite, don't instruct. Never spell out what the reader must say.
  - Everything in this file describes a shape. Never reuse its wording, or another page's.
</phrase>

<show>
  - Usually no photographs. The premises or the storefront only where it helps someone find it.
  - A map sits beside an address written out as text, never instead of it. On a phone the text comes first.
</show>

<links>
From: the header and the footer of every page. To: each location page, and the booking tool.
</links>

<industry>
Trades: emergency hours and the areas served. Clinics: booking, new-patient forms, the insurance
question. Restaurants: reservations and the phone, with hours. Professional services: a consultation
route and how quickly someone replies.
</industry>

<variant name="sent" label="after a form is sent" mode="retrieval">
The page a reader lands on after sending a form. It says the message arrived, who will reply and
how soon when the brand says, the phone number for anything urgent, and one route back into the site.
Nothing else: no second form, no pitch.
</variant>

</page_type>
