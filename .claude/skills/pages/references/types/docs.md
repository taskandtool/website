<page_type name="docs" mode="retrieval" goals="unblock" nav="full + search">

<job>
Get the reader unstuck: help that is easy to search, focused on their task, in concrete steps, and
no bigger than it needs to be. For a small business this is mostly how-tos and
reference: hours, policies, care instructions, preparation, FAQs.
</job>

<reader>
The task they are trying to finish, and where they arrived from; usually search, so each page must
make sense on its own.
</reader>

<inventory>
  tasks       how-tos from the company's own text
  reference   facts laid out the way the thing itself is structured
  explanation why things are the way they are, where the company says
  questions   the company's FAQ, in customers' words
</inventory>

<select>
  - One type per page: tutorial (learn by doing), how-to (finish a task), reference (facts),
    explanation (why). Mixing them is the root of most documentation problems.
  - Each page stands alone and says what it covers in its first line.
  - Write an FAQ only when readers need it, not for search features.
</select>

<sections>
In page order. `core` is on nearly every page of this type; everything else is a choice made
against the material. Each line says what the band holds, then when it earns its place.

  summary     core      One line on what the page covers.
  content     core      Numbered steps for a task; labels and values for reference.
  related     optional  Links to the other kinds of page rather than pasting them in.
                        When: a reader of this page usually needs one of them next.
</sections>

<phrase>
  - Task headings start with a verb; concept headings are noun phrases.
  - Each numbered step opens with an imperative verb.
  - Questions are written the way a customer would ask them.
  - Everything in this file describes a shape. Never reuse its wording, or another page's.
</phrase>

<show>
  - A picture only where it identifies what a step refers to (a part, a screen).
</show>

</page_type>
