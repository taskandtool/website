<page_type name="legal" mode="retrieval" goals="state the terms" nav="full">

<job>
The privacy policy, terms, returns and shipping, cookies, an accessibility statement, and the notices
a profession requires. These pages state the company's own terms. They are never written here: the
text comes from the owner or their lawyer, verbatim. The page's job is to make those terms findable
and readable.
</job>

<reader>
Someone checking one term (can I return this, what do you do with my data) or a regulator checking
that the notice is there. They search the page for one answer.
</reader>

<exists>
Every site needs a privacy policy. Terms, returns and shipping go on a site that sells online. A
clinic needs the notices its jurisdiction requires. Where `public/` and `brand/` has no text for a required
page, the page is a NEED. We never draft legal terms.
</exists>

<inventory>
  text        the company's own legal text, verbatim, with its date
  summary     a plain-language summary, only where the company supplies or approves one
  contact     who to contact about the policy
</inventory>

<select>
  - The owner's text, verbatim. Headings may be added to make it scannable, and nothing else changes.
  - The date it was last updated, as the text gives it.
</select>

<sections>
In page order. `core` is on every legal page; the summary is a choice.

  summary     optional  A plain-language summary of the key terms.
                        When: the owner supplies or approves it.
  text        core      The terms, verbatim, under headings, with a list of the headings linked at the top when long.
  updated     core      When it was last updated.
  contact     core      Who to ask about it.
</sections>

<phrase>
  - Nothing is paraphrased. Headings name the topic plainly.
</phrase>

<show>
  - No photographs. The measure matters: comfortable line length for long text.
</show>

<links>
From: the footer of every page, and the checkout or form it governs.
</links>

</page_type>
