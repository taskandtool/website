<page_type name="location" mode="retrieval" goals="is this place right for me, and how do I get there" nav="full">

<job>
One place: a branch, an office, a clinic, a shop, or an area a service business genuinely works in.
The reader asks whether it is near them, whether it is open, how to get there, and what this one
offers. A location page exists only because some facts are true of this place and no other.
Google names pages that differ only by the city name as doorway abuse.
</job>

<reader>
Someone choosing which location, or checking this one before they go. Usually on a phone, often
arriving from a map or a search with the town's name in it.
</reader>

<exists>
Decide first whether the page should exist. A place with its own address, hours or staff gets a page.
A service area gets one only when `public/` and `brand/` holds facts for that place: jobs done there, reviews
from there, photographs there, a service offered only there. Otherwise the places are named on the
homepage, the service pages or a single areas list. Never make near-identical pages with the town
name swapped.
</exists>

<inventory>
  identity    the location's name, full address exactly as on the Google profile, phone
  hours       opening hours, holiday hours, exceptions
  getting     map link, parking, transit, entrance, accessibility
  here        services, menu or stock that differ at this location; staff who work here
  proof       reviews that mention this place; photographs of this place and of jobs nearby
</inventory>

<select>
  - Address, phone and hours match the Google profile character for character.
  - Only facts true of this place. Anything true of every location belongs on another page.
  - The staff here, named, beat a general team line.
</select>

<sections>
In page order. `core` is on nearly every location page; everything else is a choice made against
the material. Each line says what the band holds, then when it earns its place.

  place       core      The location's name, full address, phone, and the action (book, order, call).
  hours       core      Opening hours, with holiday hours and exceptions.
  getting     optional  Map link, parking, transit, entrance, accessibility.
                        When: customers come here. Core for a storefront.
  here        optional  What is offered here that differs from elsewhere.
                        When: something does differ. It is what justifies the page.
  people      optional  The staff at this location, named, with photographs.
                        When: the brand has them.
  photographs  optional  The premises, inside and out, and work done nearby. When: the business's photographs have them.
  proof       optional  Reviews that mention this place. When: they exist.
  nearby      optional  The neighbourhoods or towns this location serves, named.
                        When: it serves an area beyond its door.
</sections>

<phrase>
  - Labels and values. The address is written out in full, never only as a map.
  - The place name is the heading. No invented local colour.
  - Everything in this file describes a shape. Never reuse its wording, or another page's.
</phrase>

<show>
  - A photograph of the outside, so a visitor recognises it when they arrive, where the business's photographs have one.
</show>

<links>
Up to the locations index. From the contact page and the footer.
</links>

<variant name="index" label="locations index" mode="retrieval">
Every location with its address, phone and today's hours, each linking to its page. With more than
about eight locations, add a search by town or postcode. A map is optional for a few locations.
Order the locations by whatever the reader uses to choose, usually the town.
</variant>

</page_type>
