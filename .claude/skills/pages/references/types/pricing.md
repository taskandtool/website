<page_type name="pricing" mode="retrieval, with some persuasion" goals="self-selection" nav="full">

<job>
The reader picks the option that fits them and knows what it will really cost. Price is the
information buyers most want; when it is missing they go to a competitor, and a hidden price reads
as expensive and secretive.
</job>

<reader>
Who is comparing, what they are comparing against, and which option most of them need.
</reader>

<inventory>
  prices      every real price, range or typical-case price, with its unit and tax status
  options     each package or tier, and who it is for
  included    what each option includes, and what it does not
  fees        extras, minimums, call-out charges, anything added later
  questions   the company's own answers about paying, cancelling, changing
</inventory>

<select>
  - A real number wherever the brand has one. Where prices vary, prices for typical cases.
    With no number in the folder, a NEED line: never "contact for pricing" as if it were an answer.
  - The differences between options stated outright; implied differences cause wrong choices.
  - Only the attributes a buyer weighs; not every fact in a table.
  - The whole cost up front: fees, minimums, what is extra and what is not included.
</select>

<sections>
In page order. `core` is on nearly every page of this type; everything else is a choice made
against the material. Each line says what the band holds, then when it earns its place.

  options     core      Each option with its price, unit, tax status and who it is for.
  included    core      What each option includes, and what it does not.
  compare     optional  A comparison table. When: two to five real alternatives with shared attributes.
  fees        optional  Extras, minimums, call-out charges, deposits.
                        When: they exist. When they do, they are never left for later.
  financing   optional  Payment plans or financing, with terms. When: the brand offers them.
  guarantee   optional  Refunds, warranties, cancellation. When: the brand states them.
  questions   optional  The company's own answers about paying, cancelling, changing.
                        When: the brand has answered them in its own text.
  action      core      The next step for each option.
</sections>

<phrase>
  - Labels and values, not argument. Name each option by who it is for or what it covers, not an
    invented tier name.
  - Every price carries its unit and whether tax is included. Terms an outsider would not know
    are defined where they appear.
  - Everything in this file describes a shape. Never reuse its wording, or another page's.
</phrase>

<show>
  - Usually no photographs; the table is the visual. A photograph only where the options differ
    visibly (materials, rooms).
</show>

<links>
In from the service pages and homepage; out to the matching service page and to booking or contact.
</links>

<industry>
Where the law requires price disclosure it is correctness, not choice: total price including
mandatory fees for US lodging; price information for listed services for UK solicitors
(industries.md).
</industry>

</page_type>
