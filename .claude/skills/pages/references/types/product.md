<page_type name="product" mode="persuasion and retrieval" goals="one" nav="full">

<job>
A product page answers every question a shopper would ask across the counter, then lets them buy.
Most product pages fail basic questions: total cost, returns, variants, scale.
</job>

<reader>
Who buys this item, for what use, and what they compare it with.
</reader>

<inventory>
  identity    name, price, unit price where quantities differ, variants and their stock
  highlights  the few features that decide the purchase
  specs       the full facts as labels and values
  cost        shipping and tax estimate, returns policy
  images      several kinds, including one that shows scale; customer photographs
  proof       reviews with count and platform
  related     alternatives that do the same job; add-ons that go with it
</inventory>

<select>
  - The total cost and the returns policy sit near the buy button.
  - Variants are visible choices, with unavailable ones shown as such, not hidden in a menu.
  - A few feature highlights beat a block of text or a long bullet list; the full specs live
    separately.
  - Every value in the product's markup is visible on the page.
</select>

<sections>
In page order. `core` is on nearly every page of this type; everything else is a choice made
against the material. Each line says what the band holds, then when it earns its place.

  buy         core      Name, price, variants as visible choices with stock, the action; delivery
                        cost or estimate and the returns policy beside the button.
  images      core      Several views, including one that shows scale and one in use.
  highlights  core      The few features that decide it.
  specs       core      Labels and values.
  reviews     optional  Reviews with count and platform; customer photographs.
                        When: reviews exist. Core once they do.
  sizing      optional  A size or fit guide. When: the product comes in sizes.
  questions   optional  The seller's own answers about this product. When: real questions exist.
  related     optional  Two separate rows: alternatives, and things that go with it.
                        When: the business's photographs have real alternatives or add-ons for this item.
</sections>

<phrase>
  - The seller's own product names and facts. Descriptions name what it is and does, not adjectives.
  - Everything in this file describes a shape. Never reuse its wording, or another page's.
</phrase>

<show>
  - Images carry the most weight on the page. One must show scale. Customer photographs are proof.
</show>

<variant name="index" label="product list" mode="retrieval">
Find and compare. Each item: image, name, price and unit price, rating with count, its variants
grouped as one item. Sorting by price, rating, best-selling and newest; a filter for each attribute
shown, scaled to the size of the catalogue (a small, well-grouped list may need none).
</variant>

</page_type>
