<page_type name="reviews" mode="persuasion" goals="credibility transfer" nav="full">

<job>
Several customers, in their own words, about real jobs, so a reader who doubts the company can check
it. Reviews work best beside the claim they prove, so they are placed on the homepage and service
pages first. A page of their own is worth making when there are many (twenty or more real ones) or
the category is decided by reviews (trades, clinics, venues). Readers suspect on-site reviews are
curated and cross-check them elsewhere, so the page points to where they can be checked. Google
has not shown stars for a business's own reviews since 2019. This page is for readers, not for
search features.
</job>

<angle>
Decide per page and write in the brief's frontmatter, from this brand's own material.

  reader  someone close to choosing, checking whether other people like them were happy.
  pain    will this company do right by me: turn up, do it well, charge what they said.
  offer   the volume, recency and specifics of what customers say, on platforms the reader trusts.
  lead    usually the aggregate rating with platform and count, then the reviews.
</angle>

<inventory>
  aggregate   each platform's rating with its count and date
  reviews     each review verbatim with the name as given, date, platform, and the job it describes
  themes      what customers praise again and again: punctuality, cleanliness, a named person
  responses   the company's replies to reviews, especially to critical ones
  links       where the reader can read them all or leave one
</inventory>

<select>
  - Named, dated reviews with their platform beat anonymous ones. "J., happy customer" is cut.
  - Reviews that name the job or the person beat general praise.
  - Range across services and years beats ten about the same thing.
  - A critical review with a good reply is kept if the brand shows one. Only five-star reviews read as curated.
  - The count on the page is the platform's count, never a number worked out by hand.
</select>

<sections>
In page order. `core` is on nearly every reviews page; everything else is a choice made against
the material. Each line says what the band holds, then when it earns its place.

  summary     core      Each platform's rating with count, and a link to read them there.
  reviews     core      The chosen reviews, verbatim, each with name, date, platform and the job.
  by service  optional  Reviews grouped under the service they are about.
                        When: there are enough for three or more services to have several each.
  people      optional  Reviews that name staff, with the role the reviewer gives.
                        When: customers name people. It is strong proof the people are real.
  replies     optional  The company's reply beneath a review. When: the brand has replies.
  leave one   optional  Where a customer can leave a review. When: the brand has the link.
  action      core      The action, for the reader who is now convinced.
</sections>

<phrase>
  - Reviews are quoted, never paraphrased or corrected, and never turned into headings.
  - Headings name what the reviews are about, plainly.
  - Everything in this file describes a shape. Never reuse its wording, or another page's.
</phrase>

<show>
  - Customer photographs where the platform carries them and the brand has them.
  - Platform marks as the marks, with the count beside them.
  - Every review with its platform's mark and its stars (SKILL.md, "Reviews, on any page").
</show>

<links>
From: the homepage and service pages, next to the reviews they show. To: each platform, and the services reviewed.
</links>

<industry>
Clinics: identifiable patient testimonials need written authorisation in the US (HIPAA).
Professional services: testimonials must not promise results and must disclose any connection.
Both are covered in industries.md.
</industry>
