<page_type name="work" mode="persuasion" goals="prove it with a real job" nav="full">

<job>
One real job, told so a buyer can see themselves in it. It says who it was for, what was wrong, what
the company did, and what changed. This covers the case study, the project page and the before-and-after.
For a considered purchase it is the strongest proof a site can carry. Without an outcome it
is only a story.
</job>

<angle>
Decide per page and write in the brief's frontmatter, from this brand's own material.

  reader  a buyer whose problem resembles this client's, by industry, size or kind of job.
  pain    the problem as the client had it, before.
  offer   what was done, and the result, with numbers where the brand has them.
  lead    usually the result, then the problem it solved.
</angle>

<inventory>
  client      name (or a description when it may not be named), industry, size, place
  problem     what was wrong, in the client's or the company's words
  work        what was done, the scope, how long it took, the services used
  result      what changed, with figures and their source
  pictures    photographs of this job: before, during, after
  voice       a quote from the client, with name and role
</inventory>

<select>
  - A named client with a role beats an anonymous one. A figure with a source beats an adjective.
  - Photographs of this job only. A good photograph of a different job is not evidence for this one.
  - A result the brand cannot back is cut, not softened.
</select>

<sections>
In page order. `core` is on nearly every work page; everything else is a choice made against the
material. Each line says what the band holds, then when it earns its place.

  summary     core      The client, the job and the result, in a few lines.
  problem     core      What was wrong before.
  work        core      What the company did, named as the services it sells.
  result      core      What changed, with numbers and their source where the brand has them.
  pictures    optional  Before, during and after, captioned. When: the business's photographs have them.
                        For a trade or a remodel this band carries the page.
  voice       optional  The client's own words, with name and role. When: the brand has them.
  facts       optional  Scope, timeline, budget band, the team. When: the brand states them.
  related     optional  Other jobs of the same kind, or the service page.
                        When: the site has them.
  action      core      The action, framed for someone with the same problem.
</sections>

<phrase>
  - Headings say what happened, plainly. No "journey" and no "partnership".
  - Numbers carry their unit, their source and their period.
  - Everything in this file describes a shape. Never reuse its wording, or another page's.
</phrase>

<show>
  - The photographs are the evidence: real, of this job, captioned with what and where.
  - Before-and-after pairs are shown at the same size and framing where the photographs allow.
</show>

<links>
To: the services used, named. From: those service pages, beside the claim this job proves.
</links>

<variant name="index" label="portfolio, gallery or case-study index" mode="retrieval">
Every job, each with a photograph, what it was, where, and a link to its page where it has one.
Filter by kind of job or service when there are enough to need it. A gallery is captioned: a grid
of uncaptioned pictures proves nothing. Supplier or stock images never appear. There is always a
route to enquire. A gallery with too few real photographs is not made at all, and the work goes
on the service pages instead.
</variant>

<industry>
Trades and remodelling: before-and-after photographs and where the job was. Professional services and
agencies: the client, the problem and a measured result. Stay within the rules on results claims
(industries.md). Venues: the event, the layout and the numbers.
</industry>

</page_type>
